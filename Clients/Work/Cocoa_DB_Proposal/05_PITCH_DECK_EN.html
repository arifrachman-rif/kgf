The constraint

Indonesia does not need more grinding capacity

Eleven Indonesian processors hold over 750,000 tonnes per year of installed capacity and run it at roughly 60% for want of local beans. Roughly 300,000 tonnes sits idle. A proposal offering more capacity has to explain itself; a proposal solving supply does not.

The model

Fermentation station → mini plant → industrial plant

Three tracks, run in parallel rather than in sequence. Tracks A and B produce evidence, product and revenue while Track C is still being financed and built.

Track A · Upstream

Fermentation stations

District centres inside managed farm areas. Cash purchase of wet bean on the spot.

  • Layer 1 — Lampung, short-radius wet bean
  • Layer 2 — PBPH forestry concessions nationally, shipping dry fermented bean
Track B · Interim

Pesawaran mini plant

Existing, idle, managed by Disperindag Pesawaran. Reactivated, not built.

  • R&D and formulation
  • Initial production for market test
  • Scale up from a working baseline
Track C · Downstream

Industrial plant

Sized to provable supply, specified to buyer preference — not to a vendor catalogue.

  • Machinery under Euler Hermes cover
  • Commissioning Q1 2031
Running in parallel from day one
Certification. Naturland organic and Naturland Fair. The 36-month conversion clock starts in Phase 0, not later.
Market development. Quiet buyer approach using physical product from Track B. LOI, then contract.
Plant preparation. Feasibility study, permitting and ECA application run early — construction waits for the gate.
Capacity is sized from provable supply, never the reverse. Only written commitments count as supply.

Why the design holds

Fermentation must be local. Aggregation may be national.

Pods rest up to three days after harvest before being broken. But once a pod is opened, the wet beans must be in the fermentation box within eight hours — beyond about a day outside, moisture is lost, unwanted microbes take hold and fermentation fails. That clock is a physical limit, not a cost preference.

Central facility & plots it can serve Plots outside the window District centre & its catchment

Whole pods · up to 3 days · travels

Pod storage before breaking is standard practice and improves fermentation. This is what makes a district centre viable rather than one per village — pods can be gathered across a real catchment.

Wet beans · 8 hours · cannot travel

The binding limit. Wet beans cannot be hauled onward, so one central facility for all of Lampung is physically impossible. Centres are built where pods are opened, and each is added only after the previous one proves volume and quality — milestone-linked drawdown, not a lump sum.

Dried below 7% · stable · travels

Once fermented and dried the bean is stable, which is what makes Layer 2 legitimate: every concession must build its own fermentation. Each centre is one geolocated point with a defined plot catchment, so plot → centre → plant is short and provable, and TAPAK already records it.

The root cause of low fermentation in Lampung is incentive, not technique. A centre in the farmer's own district means cash on the spot, so the fermentation differential reaches the farmer instead of being promised at season end.

The cheapest CAPEX in the programme

Throughput is constrained by one or two pieces of equipment, not by the process. Relieving them lifts output from an already-commissioned line — small, precisely scoped, no commissioning risk, measurable within months.

We are not seeking finance to prove our cocoa processing capability. We are seeking a finance partner who will grow alongside our farmers and our cocoa industry — because partner matters.
The facility is government-held: it is presented to the bank as evidence and cash flow, never as security. By the time machinery is ordered under ECA cover we hold actual yields, actual flavour profiles and buyer feedback on product of our own manufacture.

Supply security · the bank's second condition

Sized from what we can prove, not from what we would like

A 5,000 tonne plant at the 60% utilisation condition needs 3,000 tonnes of certified fermented bean a year. Our current Lampung base does not reach that alone — so we say so, and show what closes it.

Provable today, Lampung base To be closed by Layer 2, written commitments only

The rule that governs the table

Land potential is not supply. Cooperative membership is not supply. A concession's hectarage is not supply. Only a written commitment enters the count — the same standard by which we rejected the predecessor proposal's claim of 7,500 farmers.

Why Layer 2 is worth more than tonnage

Harvest peaks differ by island, so cross-island supply levels plant intake through the year and lifts achievable utilisation directly. Weather risk diversifies. And a licensed concession carries permits, boundary maps and legality documents by regulation — the strongest EUDR position available.

What the bank is asked to read

Not a projection — a purchasing ledger. Volume per month, price actually paid, quality actually delivered, per plot, in TAPAK, with dashboard access available to an outside party for independent verification.

Chain: capacity 5,000 t × 60% utilisation = 3,000 t required · one mature fermentation node ≈ 500 t/yr · 6 nodes · wet-bean intake ≈ 7,500 t. Design case takes the conservative bound throughout; the upside case runs to 7,500 t capacity.

The differentiator

The project earns revenue throughout construction

Every greenfield cocoa project has a period between financial close and commissioning in which it earns nothing and services debt out of reserves. This one does not.

Track A revenue Track B revenue Track C revenue Track C construction, no revenue
Our own gates, stated so the committee can see they are ours. No Phase 2 without a signed LOI. No financial close without contracts covering more than 60% of planned capacity. If the Phase 0 figures do not lock, Phase 1 does not begin — the same discipline we used to reject the predecessor proposal, applied to ourselves.
Conservative assumptions throughout: 36-month organic conversion, ECA process 9–18 months to financial close, construction 24 months. Feasibility study and permitting are pulled forward into Phase 1, and the conversion clock starts in Phase 0.

Financing structure & the ask

German public money upstream opens German machinery exports downstream

Upstream — develoPPP (BMZ, via GIZ)

Conditional grant, maximum EUR 2 million, matched 50:50. Funds Track A: fermentation centres, farmer training, ICS, certification, EUDR and LkSG compliance systems.

Application window closes 30 September 2026. Ideas competition runs twice a year — missing it moves the upstream funding a full semester.

Downstream — Deutsche Bank under ECA cover

Senior debt for the industrial plant, machinery covered by Euler Hermes (Federal Republic of Germany); raw-material working capital under Swiss PO finance (SERV).

Precedent exists: Euler Hermes cover for J.M. Voith machinery exported to Indonesia, disbursed by DZ Bank on a 10-year tenor. Euler Hermes' flex&cover revision works in this structure's favour.

The argument runs both ways. To BMZ: this upstream investment is the precondition for a much larger industrial investment financed under German export credit guarantee, while securing EUDR- and LkSG-compliant supply for German industry. To the bank: the upstream is co-financed by BMZ and implemented with GIZ, so supply-security risk falls not because we assert it, but because a German public funder has examined and funded it.
