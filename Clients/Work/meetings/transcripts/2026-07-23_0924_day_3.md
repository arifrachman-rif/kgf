## Executive Summary
The third day of the workshop focused on embedding Triple Bottom Line (People, Planet, Profit) principles into supply chain operations, environmental conservation practices, social compliance, and export marketing strategies. Participants analyzed key industry challenges, including high raw material price volatility, European Union regulatory compliance costs, and groundwater protection mandates. The session concluded with practical group exercises on creating sustainability pitch decks, targeting buyer personas, and leveraging Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) frameworks for green financing.

---

## Discussion

### Multi-Stakeholder Sustainability Concepts and Triple Bottom Line (3P)
The facilitator and participants reviewed the nested Triple Bottom Line model, emphasizing that the economy operates as a subsidiary of society, which remains entirely constrained by ecological limits. Discussions highlighted dynamics within the coffee supply chain in Tanggamus, Lampung, where direct sourcing by multinational buyers previously failed without engaging local multi-stakeholder platforms and large exporters. The group addressed urgent structural challenges in agriculture, including aging farmer demographics (over 50 years old), soil degradation from long-term chemical usage, and the transition toward regenerative practices driven by European market mandates for traceably low pesticide levels.

### Environmental Sustainability Practices (Planet)
Participants examined industrial environmental management with a focus on water efficiency, energy transitions, and waste reduction. Representative examples from carrageenan processing demonstrated that sourcing cleaner raw materials reduces initial washing water volumes, while companies utilize river water processing and 50 percent wastewater recycling systems. Strict regulations in industrial areas like Bekasi enforce 150-meter deep recharge wells to counter groundwater depletion. The discussion also covered energy transitions from fossil fuels to piped natural gas (PGN) or biomass, alongside regional price disparities and rigid quota penalties. Additionally, global circular economy practices were highlighted, such as combining coffee cherry husks with seaweed processing byproducts to create organic fertilizer.

### Social Sustainability and Worker Welfare (People)
The session detailed workplace standards, auditing procedures, and community engagement. Marine Stewardship Council (MSC) certification for shrimp fisheries requires strict net mesh regulations, minimum catch sizes, and two-week auditor vessel shadowing. Industrial compliance requirements mandate paying regional minimum wages (UMR), providing BPJS social security, prohibiting child labor, and ensuring on-time payment for overtime. Further examples of corporate social responsibility included post-retirement training programs, such as Moringa cultivation initiatives, and 6 to 9-month educational internships in partnership with vocational colleges earning academic credits.

### Financial Realities, Trade Barriers, and Regulatory Costs (Profit)
The financial constraints of operating sustainably were discussed thoroughly. Recent seaweed price spikes from IDR 10,000 to IDR 35,000 per kilogram caused multiple carrageenan processing plants to pause operations. Similarly, coconut shell charcoal briquette costs surged due to raw coconut export demand from China. Exporting to European markets imposes heavy compliance costs, including mandatory Food Contact Material (FCM) testing, PFAS chemical restriction testing, and UN-approved packaging. Because local Indonesian laboratories currently lack recognition for these tests, exporters face high costs for overseas certification. Bonded zone (Kawasan Berikat) import incentives were noted as a key tool to offset machinery import costs against mandatory export quotas.

### Sustainability Marketing, Branding, and GRI Framework
The final segment addressed sustainability communication, consumer segmentation, and reporting frameworks funded by external initiatives like SECO. Green loan options from financial institutions increasingly require GRI-standard sustainability reporting. Participants developed pitch deck frameworks identifying Unique Selling Propositions (USPs), such as 100 percent coconut shell briquettes with 3-plus hour burn times, zero sparks, and low ash content paired with 24/7 customer service. Marketing strategies distinguished between price-sensitive general market segments and demographic segments willing to pay premium prices for certified organic, healthy, and socially responsible products.
